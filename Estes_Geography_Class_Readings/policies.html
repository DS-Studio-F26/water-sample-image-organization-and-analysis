Course Policies

The policies for the class regarding grading, attendance, communications, academic integrity, AI, and University policies related to accessibility, FERPA, and Title IX are provided below.

Assessment

Grades in this course will assessed according to four components:

  • Participation: Determined based on attendance (see next section), contributions to discussion, in-class problem-solving, quizzes, and question sets related to the reading;
  • Assignments: There will be three practical coding-based assignments during the semester;
  • Quizzes and exams: We will give in-class, closed-notes, pen/pencil and paper 1) coding quizzes (at least one) and 2) a pre-project conceptual exam;
  • Project plan: A plan describing how your final project team will undertake the final project;
  • Final project report: The final code, analyses, and report for your team project.

Each component will be weighted as follows in determining the final grade:

Component DSCI215 GEOG315
Participation 10% 10%
Assignments (3) 30% 25%
Quizzes and exam 25% 20%
Final project overview 5% 5%
Final group project 30% 40%

Attendance

Attendance is mandatory. If you need to miss a class, please arrange with one of the instructors in advance. There will be frequent quizzes related to concepts in the assigned reading. These will count towards participation grades.

Another part of attendance is being present when in class. Present means you are paying attention to instructors and not looking at your phone or laptop. There is a difference between looking at your laptop for taking notes, and browsing on your laptop. It is very easy to tell the difference from the front of the class room. If you spend most of your time in class just looking at your screen, that will count as an absence.

Communications & Organization

  • Class communication will be handled via a dedicated Slack channel. The instructors will not use or reply to email.
  • Class material will be handled through a combination of GitHub, Slack, and Google Drive.

Academic Integrity

The University’s policies on academic integrity will be strictly enforced. Plagiarism, cheating, lying, stealing, or falsification will not be tolerated and will be referred to the University Administration. Please go here to learn more about this policy and what it entails.

Technology

In class

The in-person parts of the course are divided into lecture and lab. Lectures will be tech free, pen/pencil and paper note-taking, no phone or laptops (exceptions are of course allowed for accommodations), except for the 2-3 (maybe 4) weeks, when we start doing final projects.

We know this policy might seem odd given that this is a class organized around computation, but there is a fair bit of conceptual material to learn, which in many ways is the most important content, particularly as coding is increasingly handled by agents (see next section). My experience teaching this course to date is that laptops and phones prove to be too much of a distraction (for some, not all), and the conceptual parts are not well absorbed. The conceptual parts are what help you know whether the model or approach you are choosing is appropriate for the task to which you want to apply it.

There are other reasons to adopt a tech-free lecture policy, which are summarized in this New York Times opinion piece from last November. Here are some key passages:

It seems ridiculous to have to say this, but digital distraction is terrible for academic performance. The more time college students spent doing something else on their laptops during class, the lower their exam scores, even after accounting for academic ability.

And regarding notes:

Across 24 studies, college students who took handwritten notes were 58 percent more likely to get A’s in their courses than those who typed notes on laptops. In contrast, students who typed notes were 75 percent more likely to fail the course than those who wrote them by hand.

Lab will be hands-on coding, and will require your laptops.

Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI)

This is a class devoted to learning about the models that underpin AI, and AI-assisted coding is increasingly used by model developers and practitioners as an integral part of their workflow. We will allow and even encourage the use of AI assistants in developing assignments and even your final project. However, we expect the following from you: 1) you show how you used AI as your, which includes documentation of all prompts and responses; 2) that you understand the code you are writing, and do not completely outsource the code yourself. You can code up a complete machine learning pipeline, including a working neural network, quite quickly and run it, but you should understand the model and why it behaves the way it does, which means you have to understand your code. Coding knowledge will be demonstrated by your ability to arrive at correct solutions in your assignments (code completely outsourced to AI will often, if not usually, fail, depending on complexity), and through your grades on a pen and paper coding quiz that we will give. We also reserve the right to give impromptu coding quizzes.

For any writing that you do, e.g. answering quiz and exam questions, writing introductions, methods sections, results, discussions, or other parts of final projects or assignments, AI use is not permitted. Please note that it is very easy for us to tell when it is and isn’t used for writing.

Student Accessibility Services

Clark University is committed to providing students with documented disabilities equal access to all university programs and facilities. Students are encouraged to register with Student Accessibility Services (SAS) to explore and access accommodations that may support their success in their coursework. SAS is located on the second floor of the Shaich Family Alumni and Student Engagement Center (ASEC). Please contact SAS at accessibilityservices@clarku.edu with questions or to initiate the registration process. For additional information, please visit the SAS website.

Title IX

Please be aware that all Clark University faculty and teaching assistants are considered responsible employees, which means that if you tell us about a situation involving the aforementioned offenses, we must share that information with the Title IX Coordinator, Brittany Brickman (titleix@clarku.edu). Although we must make that notification, you will, for the most part, control how your case will be handled, including whether or not you wish to pursue a formal complaint. Our goal is to make sure you are aware of the range of options available to you and have access to the resources you need.

If you wish to speak to a confidential resource who does not have this reporting responsibility, you can contact Clark’s Center for Counseling and Professional Growth (508-793-7678), Clark’s Health Center (508-793-7467), or confidential resource providers on campus (see here for a list, and for other resources).
